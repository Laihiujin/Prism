# Composition patterns

Choose one primary pattern based on what proves the video's promise fastest.

## 1. Editorial collage

Best for template packs, resource collections, UI showcases, and list-style content.

- Top 35–45%: short headline with one emphasized number or keyword.
- Bottom 55–65%: real screenshots or a real collage.
- Transition: a separate background-color-to-transparent gradient overlapping the collage by roughly 8–16% of canvas height.
- Optional subject: portrait cutout at lower-right, covering no more than one-third of the proof area.

The gradient exists to protect headline readability. It must not replace, repaint, or fabricate the screenshots beneath it.

## 2. Face-led high-CTR

Best when the presenter, emotion, or personal result is the hook.

- Subject occupies about 35–50% of the canvas.
- Head and expression stay large enough to read in a small feed preview.
- Title uses 3–8 words where the language permits.
- One outline or shadow separates the subject; avoid multiple glow effects.
- Supporting screenshots remain secondary and real.

Do not generate a substitute face. Use the supplied portrait and verify facial identity after background removal.

## 3. Proof-led product/UI

Best when a real screen, result, chart, or before/after is more persuasive than a face.

- One proof asset is dominant.
- Crop to the meaningful result rather than shrinking an entire unreadable interface.
- Use arrows, circles, or labels sparingly—usually one annotation is enough.
- Keep logos and data accurate.

## 4. YouTube cinematic split

Best for 16:9 comparison, reaction, challenge, and transformation topics.

- Strong left/right contrast or foreground/background depth.
- One large face or object and one concise hook.
- Avoid placing important content in the lower-right duration-badge area.
- Test at approximately 10–15% of full size.

## 5. Creator Impact

Best for dense vertical AI tutorials, product tests, beginner explainers, and strong conversational questions. Read [signal-impact-system.md](signal-impact-system.md) for the flagship signatures, palettes, and delivery gate.

- Headline occupies roughly 35–45% of a vertical canvas. Use two or three tightly stacked pale-yellow/white lines with a very thick dark keyline; the hook must remain readable at 25% scale.
- Portrait occupies 35–55%, with the eyes and mouth unobstructed. Let the cutout overlap the headline or proof slightly so the groups feel connected, but do not cover key words or data.
- Real proof occupies 15–30%. Use one meaningful crop rather than a full-page screenshot.
- Keep title, portrait, and proof within one compact visual triangle. A central gap larger than 12% of canvas height is a failed composition, not useful negative space.
- Use one thick hand-drawn arrow and up to three green check items to connect the hook, person, large count, and proof.
- Extend a real topic interface, room, benchmark, or product screen into the background. Do not replace usable context with an unrelated abstract field.
- Inspect the portrait mask on both a light and dark field. Any leftover rectangular background, black halo, clipped hat/hair, or broken shoulder edge must be fixed before delivery.

## Variant strategy

When the user wants alternatives, create variants with different hypotheses:

1. **Text-led:** the promise or number carries the click.
2. **Face-led:** expression and identity carry the click.
3. **Proof-led:** the real product, screenshot, or result carries the click.

Keep the factual claim constant across variants so click performance compares composition rather than different promises.
