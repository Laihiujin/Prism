# Platform concepts and multiple versions

These are editorial starting points. The user's own same-brand references and the actual content come first; this is not a fixed per-platform template.

| Platform | Why people watch | Possible visual mechanisms | Title ↔ cover relationship |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Douyin | Grasp the hook instantly and decide to stay | A single action moment, a close viewpoint, a clear contrast, a strong visual question | The title names the subject; the cover focuses on one short hook. Don't invent surprise or outcomes. |
| WeChat Channels | Trust and recognition in everyday situations | A real relationship between people and the object, a life moment, restrained branding | Easy to understand and to pass on; the short title stands alone; information, not emotional hard-sell. |
| Bilibili | Concrete content worth understanding further | Evidence-based detail close-ups, process/structure relationships, work information | Subject + question/method/work. No "tested" without a test; no fake internals without a teardown. |
| Xiaohongshu | Fits a scenario, searchable, worth saving | Tutorial first image, evidence of real use, clear information cards | Subject + scenario + concrete value. Don't fake first-hand experience; keep series visually consistent. |

## Concept card

For each platform record: content basis, viewer motivation, promise, cover copy, hero visual, scene, camera, text zone, brand zone, source-image list, product-logic check result, how it differs from the other concepts, and acceptance criteria.

Keep the visual hierarchy of logo, title and product separate. A logo is brand identification; it should not automatically become the largest title.

## Difference check

Remove the text and logo: can you still tell the platforms apart? If only color or product angle distinguishes them, propose a new concept.

Two versions can't differ just by making one of them a night/purple scene; change the visual proposition or the information relationship. Brand color and wordmark may stay consistent.

Platform difference doesn't mean every platform needs a particular font, a person, or a catalog layout. Read the user's real references and extract patterns, not objects.

## Ratios and counts

Expand the request into variant_id × platform × ratio. Don't increase the count by default; make a representative draft first, and when the user wants to review, expand only after review.

3:4 can be 1080×1440 and 4:3 can be 1440×1080. These are exact-ratio examples, not claims about a single official platform standard. Follow the user's request and what the target uploader actually accepts.

Portrait and landscape each need their own placement of hero visual, title and safe crop zone. Confirm the originals support the viewpoint; never stretch the product or crop away key information.
